# Premium TVC Look and Texture System

“Premium” is an outcome of controlled hierarchy, credible light, differentiated materials and deliberate
restraint. It is not a list of luxury adjectives. Build the look from visible variables and acceptance checks.

Use `causal-visual-direction.md` for the source-backed tone, lighting, material, composition, atmosphere,
readability-pass and failed-generation methods that operationalize this system.

When no visual direction is approved, begin with `default-quality-routing.md`. Its shared floor defines the
minimum authored control; its selected category branch determines which product evidence, scale logic and motion
vocabulary belong in this look bible.

## 1. Brand and emotional cause

Before choosing a look, state:

- The precise emotion, including tension or contradiction—not merely positive/negative.
- The product or brand property that causes this visual world.
- The category default being avoided.
- One signature visual behaviour that remains recognisable without the logo.

Apply the competitor swap: if the same look description fits three competitors unchanged, it is generic.

## 2. Tonal architecture

Specify:

- Exposure key: low, middle or high; what is correctly exposed.
- Contrast distribution: global contrast and local subject/background separation.
- Black floor: lifted/soft, deep with retained texture, or graphic silhouette.
- Highlight behaviour: broad soft roll-off, narrow hard specular, controlled bloom or designed clipping.
- Midtone density: airy, neutral, dense or compressed—and why.
- Saturation distribution: which surfaces carry color and which remain restrained.
- Color-temperature relationship: environment, subject, product and edge separation.
- Focal color: the one color that moves attention or marks a story change.
- Separation compensation: when luminance contrast is compressed, which edge, hue/temperature, contact,
  material, composition, spatial-line or focus cue restores hierarchy.

Acceptance: grayscale still preserves hierarchy; blacks retain intended information; highlights do not turn
skin, paint, glass or metal into the same white plastic response.

## 3. Lighting geometry

Describe every meaningful light by observable behaviour:

`source motivation + direction/height + apparent size + hardness + falloff + key/fill relationship +
negative fill + edge separation + practical/environment contribution + reflection control`

All shadows, reflections and specular streaks must agree with the light geometry. Do not add rim, neon or
volumetric light without a source or dramatic function. For products, describe the shape and travel of the
highlight, not only the lamp type.

## 4. Material response

For each hero material define:

- Material family and construction.
- Microtexture and manufacturing/usage evidence.
- Roughness and gloss distribution.
- Specular width, strength, color and edge behaviour.
- Transmission/refraction for glass or translucent layers.
- Contact shadow, compression, fingerprints, condensation, dust or wear when appropriate.
- Response to movement, wind, water, skin contact and changing light.
- Surface condition derived from cleanliness, manufacturing state, maintenance and use history; pristine
  products may explicitly require zero dirt, scratches or weathering.

Reject the AI-plastic failure: leather, skin, glass, paint, metal, fabric and food must not share one generic
gloss. Grain, halation and bloom are finishing layers, not substitutes for material design.

## 5. Optical and spatial character

Specify visible optics rather than equipment prestige:

- Field-of-view and camera distance, which together define perspective.
- Spatial compression or expansion and tolerated distortion.
- Foreground occlusion, middle-ground subject and background information.
- Focus plane, depth distribution and motivated focus transfer.
- Flare/ghosting/bloom/halation only when sourced and useful.
- Edge contrast, microcontrast and sharpness distribution.
- Motion blur and shutter feel as part of movement weight.
- Atmospheric depth through haze, particles, reflection or color separation.

Shallow focus is not automatically cinematic. The product or performance must have a readable focus window.

## 6. Color and texture hierarchy

Use a hierarchy rather than a palette dump:

1. Environment/base family.
2. Subject or product separation family.
3. One focal/brand accent.
4. Optional event color used only for a transition or reveal.

Tint neutrals deliberately. Low saturation must still contain luminance, temperature and material contrast.
Avoid default blue-purple neon, black-gold luxury, full-frame teal-orange, identical cyan rims and dead neutral gray
unless the project can explain why they belong to this brand.

## 7. Temporal texture

The look must survive motion:

- Define whether light is stable, passing, pulsing, flickering or revealed by movement.
- Define how reflections travel across curved surfaces.
- Specify environmental response: fabric drag, dust wake, water inertia, foliage delay or skin micro-movement.
- Match camera acceleration and image blur to perceived mass.
- Use intentional stillness as contrast; do not animate every layer.

## 8. Look prompt block

Write in this order:

`brand/emotional cause → tonal architecture → motivated light geometry → color hierarchy → hero material
responses → optical/depth character → atmospheric/temporal texture → prohibited drift → acceptance checks`

Every adjective must resolve into at least one visible decision. If “premium”, “cinematic”, “luxury” or
“dreamlike” can be deleted without changing the instructions, delete it.

## 9. Look acceptance checks

- Brand swap test: still specific to this product/brand.
- Grayscale hierarchy test.
- Light-source consistency test across adjacent shots.
- Material differentiation test.
- Product color/geometry/logo protection test.
- Focus and readability window test.
- Anti-default test: no unexplained AI aesthetic shortcuts.
- Reference-delta test: each reference controls declared dimensions only.
- Readability-baseline test: identity, geometry, Logo, text and mandatory information survive the look delta.
- Atmosphere deletion test: remove any medium or particle that has no source, job or stopping condition.
- Default-route test: one primary branch and dominant visual engine are explicit when style was not supplied.
- Sibling-product test: automotive and smartphone scale, feature and reflection vocabularies are not substituted.
- Hold/burst test: every major product state has a readable hold and every flash/inversion/distortion event has a recovery.
