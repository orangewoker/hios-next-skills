## 导演：wes-anderson

# Wes Anderson

## Identity
- Chinese name: 韦斯·安德森
- Region: United States
- Representative works: *The Royal Tenenbaums*, *The Grand Budapest Hotel*, *The French Dispatch*
- Best suited for: family eccentricity, ritualized comedy, miniature social systems, melancholy order.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose a precise routine whose handcrafted order cannot contain grief, embarrassment, or rebellion. Comedy emerges from literal behavior inside a visibly arranged world.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: use windows, soft daylight, practical lamps, stage-like room fixtures, or exterior sun with clear even coverage.
- Shadow and contrast: favor moderate graphic contrast, readable planes, and controlled soft shadows rather than psychological darkness.
- Highlights and visibility: costumes, props, paper, painted walls, and faces remain evenly but not commercially legible.
- Reject: dramatic noir shadow, horror backlight, glossy studio beauty, or flat light with no source.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: build a limited object-based palette from costume, paint, furniture, stationery, landscape, and era.
- Skin and midtones: keep skin natural enough to belong inside the palette without plastic warmth.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: maintain clean midtones, restrained blacks, and protected pastel or saturated surfaces.
- Reject: random candy color, Instagram pastel filters, teal-orange grading, or palette detached from production objects.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: use frontal normal or modest wide views, exact lateral profiles, and occasional measured close details.
- Height and position: align with room, corridor, vehicle, stage, or façade at a physically centered or orthogonal position.
- Movement and focus: use locked tableaux, straight lateral tracks, whip-like pivots, or axial moves with clear comic timing; preserve broad focus.
- Witness role: a precise storybook stage manager, not an institutional threat; reject casual handheld and shallow romantic blur.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: organize a group ritual or solitary misfit within visible order.
- Layers and obstruction: use frames, shelves, doors, labels, furniture, and compartments as handcrafted structure.
- Blocking and geometry: favor frontal lines, profiles, symmetry, lateral entrances, and deliberate spacing, then let one behavior break rhythm.
- Information control: make the social system legible while emotion leaks through props, timing, and absence.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: choose a routine one beat after it becomes emotionally awkward.
- Character action: present, label, line up, read, wait, or leave frame laterally.
- Visual center: make one misfit gesture break visual order.
- Blocking: use precise spacing and comic timing.
- Environment: let palette, labels, rooms, and objects define the miniature system.

## Capture Texture
Use handcrafted surfaces, practical miniatures or real sets, broad focus, clean exposure, and tactile paper, fabric, paint, and wood. Avoid CGI dollhouse gloss.

## Common Misreadings
- Treating symmetry as the entire style.
- Adding random pastel colors.
- Making characters emotionless.
- Using miniature sets without social logic.
- Applying flat graphic composition to a serious scene without comic timing.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Stanley Kubrick, Anderson makes order playful and handcrafted: softer even source light, object-based limited palettes, orthogonal lateral camera, and comic frontal blocking rather than formal high-control light, restrained exact color, psychologically distancing wide perspective, and ritualized geometry pressing on unstable humans.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use object-based limited color, soft source-led graphic light, orthogonal normal-or-wide camera, broad focus, precise lateral or frontal blocking, and one emotionally awkward gesture breaking a handcrafted social order.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the scene as a precise routine becoming awkward; align the camera orthogonally with the room, build a limited palette from real objects, stage readable frontal or lateral spacing, and let one delayed gesture fracture comic order without dollhouse gloss.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: siblings inventory their closed family hotel.
- Translation: each stands behind a labeled luggage cart while the youngest quietly removes one room key; a frontal lobby view uses dusty rose walls, green uniforms, window daylight, and a straight lateral exit.

## 导演：wong-kar-wai

# Wong Kar-wai

## Identity
- Chinese name: 王家卫
- Region: Hong Kong
- Representative works: *In the Mood for Love*, *Chungking Express*, *Fallen Angels*
- Best suited for: urban solitude, missed connection, subjective time.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose an almost-encounter, repeated route, or private gesture interrupted before completion. City surfaces hold emotional delay through reflection, compression, and passing bodies.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: mix actual corridor fluorescents, shop lamps, street signs, windows, or vehicle light; no invisible beauty key.
- Shadow and contrast: let frames and passing sources produce broken local contrast, with faces entering and leaving uneven light.
- Highlights and visibility: glass, wet streets, mirrors, and metal may flare or smear selectively while part of the gesture stays unreadable.
- Reject: uniform neon glow, clean rim light, evenly lit glamour, or darkness used only as romance.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: color comes from conflicting practical sources—green fluorescent, tungsten rooms, red signage, sodium streets—not a global preset.
- Skin and midtones: allow skin to inherit mixed-source contamination and partial underexposure.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: tolerate clipped practicals, dense passages, and exposure drift during movement; keep dead neutral areas between color events.
- Reject: blanket teal-magenta, green filter, luxury perfume color, or saturation without a visible source.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: alternate compressed longer-lens intimacy with physically close wide-angle distortion when the room demands it.
- Height and position: watch from across a corridor, beside a counter, behind glass, or within a passing crowd at human height.
- Movement and focus: use motivated lateral drift, vehicle motion, selective step-print-like smear, split focus, or delayed focus; not every frame requires blur.
- Witness role: a subjective passerby caught too near or too late; reject floating music-video moves and arbitrary slow shutter.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: place an incomplete gesture, reflection, or separation between bodies at the center of attention.
- Layers and obstruction: use doorframes, mirrors, curtains, glass, counters, and passing people to fracture proximity.
- Blocking and geometry: compress bodies that cannot connect, or distort near/far distance so the city crowds intimacy without resolving it.
- Information control: keep one emotional fragment clear while faces, destinations, and secondary actions remain partial.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: select waiting, passing, or the instant just after someone fails to speak.
- Character action: hold a lift, sort change, wipe a counter, turn away, or repeat a route.
- Visual center: privilege the gap, reflection, or interrupted hand rather than a romantic portrait.
- Blocking: keep bodies close in projection but separated by frames, timing, or direction.
- Environment: make practical light and pedestrian flow interrupt the encounter.

## Capture Texture
Use tactile mixed exposure, selective motion smear, breathing focus, reflective flare, and partial detail only when caused by movement, glass, or practical lights. Avoid polished nostalgia.

## Common Misreadings
- Filling every scene with neon.
- Applying random slow-shutter blur.
- Turning longing into fashion posing.
- Using cheongsam, cigarettes, or green filters as identity tokens.
- Making every reflective surface decorative and fully legible.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Lou Ye, Wong compresses or distorts proximity into choreographed emotional delay: mixed practical color is more patterned, motion smear more selective, frames and reflections more composed, and the city stages near-encounters rather than the camera physically struggling inside unstable intimacy.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use an incomplete urban encounter, mixed practical color, broken local contrast, compressed or distorted proximity, reflective obstruction, selective motion smear, partial focus, and a subjective witness arriving too late. Do not reduce the method to neon or romance.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the scene around an interrupted private gesture; watch through a plausible reflective or obstructing layer, compress or distort bodies separated by timing, let mixed practical sources contaminate skin and exposure, and keep only one emotional fragment clear amid selective smear and dense passages.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: two tenants return home seconds apart.
- Translation: one holds the lift door then lets it close; a side witness behind a laundry rack catches the reflected eye and withdrawing hand under tired fluorescent light and apartment tungsten spill, with neither face fully available.

## 导演：yasujiro-ozu

# Yasujiro Ozu

## Identity
- Chinese name: 小津安二郎
- Region: Japan
- Representative works: *Tokyo Story*, *Late Spring*, *An Autumn Afternoon*
- Best suited for: family routine, domestic transition, quiet aftermath.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose the small domestic action after a change has already occurred. Silence, routine, and an unused place carry more weight than declaration or expressive performance.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: let windows, ceiling fixtures, table lamps, and reflected household light illuminate the room before singling out a face.
- Shadow and contrast: keep contrast gentle and planar, with calm falloff into corners and under furniture.
- Highlights and visibility: ceramic, wood, paper, and skin receive modest highlights; an empty area remains readable but not spotlighted.
- Reject: theatrical backlight, hard noir slashes, sentimental glow, or dramatic pools isolating a speaker.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: use familiar wood, painted walls, fabric, food, and daylight as quiet color blocks without a nostalgic wash.
- Skin and midtones: hold skin in stable domestic midtones that share the room's source color.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: retain soft blacks, protected window whites, and broad middle values; avoid lifted pastel haze.
- Reject: sepia memory filters, candy domestic color, aggressive cool-warm splits, or faded-film shorthand.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: use a normal-lens medium or medium-wide observation that preserves furniture and room continuity.
- Height and position: place the locked camera distinctly low but physically possible, aligned with seated domestic life rather than mechanically imitating tatami height.
- Movement and focus: remain still by default; use middle-to-deep focus and move only when the witness must relocate between rooms.
- Witness role: a patient family presence, not surveillance or an expressive participant; reject push-ins, roaming intimacy, and showy shallow focus.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: allow a quiet action and an unoccupied place to share importance.
- Layers and obstruction: use doorways, furniture edges, shelves, and adjoining rooms as stable planes rather than decorative frames.
- Blocking and geometry: separate people through ordinary household distances, seated axes, and unfinished arrangements.
- Information control: show routine clearly while withholding emotional explanation; absence may occupy more space than a face.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: choose the task immediately after departure, news, or a visit.
- Character action: return a cup, fold cloth, clear one place, or pause beside a packed object.
- Visual center: divide attention between the action and meaningful empty space.
- Blocking: keep people restrained and separated by furniture or thresholds.
- Environment: let the room preserve the missing person's trace.

## Capture Texture
Use quiet exposure, matte domestic surfaces, stable focus, and natural shadow falloff. Texture should feel lived with, never lacquered, quaint, or artificially aged.

## Common Misreadings
- Enforcing perfect symmetry in every room.
- Copying a tatami-level setup without spatial reason.
- Showing empty rooms with no narrative trace.
- Replacing restraint with sentimental crying.
- Exoticizing Japanese décor or adding nostalgic film wash.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Hirokazu Kore-eda, Ozu is lower, more locked, more planar, and more formally separated: gentler stable light, quieter material color, normal-lens room continuity, and meaningful empty geometry rather than mobile eye-level observation amid overlapping family behavior.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use a low stable normal-lens witness, restrained domestic blocking through furniture and thresholds, meaningful unoccupied space, middle-to-deep focus, modest source-based household light, familiar material color, and emotional aftermath expressed through routine.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the quiet action after a family change; observe from a low physically placeable position, keep furniture and adjoining rooms continuous, let an unused area share the visual center, and use gentle domestic light, calm midtones, matte materials, and no expressive camera move.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: packing is finished before an adult child leaves home.
- Translation: a parent returns the child's cup to an otherwise empty shelf while another waits at the doorway; a low locked normal-lens view keeps the unused table place readable under late window light and one dim practical.
